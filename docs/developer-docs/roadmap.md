# Developer Onboarding Journey & Documentation Roadmap

This roadmap defines the sequential learning and implementation path for engineers adopting NATS in this repository.

Follow this order when designing and building services. Before moving to the next step, ensure you can confidently answer the self-check questions for that guide.

---

## Roadmap Matrix

| Step | Category | Document | Scope | "Before You Move Forward" Self-Check Questions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **01** | Foundations | [NATS Overview](nats_overview.md) | High-level platform introduction, dual messaging models, platform capabilities, architecture, and terminology comparison with Solace and Kafka. | 1. What is NATS and why is it different from traditional message brokers?<br>2. Can you explain the two messaging models NATS provides and when each applies?<br>3. What capabilities does NATS offer as a platform (e.g., pub/sub, request-reply, persistence, isolation)?<br>4. How do familiar concepts from Solace or Kafka translate into NATS terminology? |
| **02** | Local Environment | [Local Setup Guide](setup/nats-local-setup-guide.md) | Running and verifying single-node and 3-node NATS clusters locally using Podman, Compose, and the NATS CLI. | 1. Can you run a local NATS server with JetStream enabled on your machine?<br>2. Do you know when to use the single-node setup versus the 3-node cluster for your testing needs?<br>3. Can you publish and subscribe to a message using the NATS CLI to confirm the environment is working? |
| **03** | Client Connectivity | [Client Guide](client/nats-client-guide.md) | Foundation for application developers: subject addressing, message structure, and SDK installation across Go, Java, and Python. | 1. How does subject-based addressing work in NATS, and how do wildcards expand what a subscriber receives?<br>2. What makes up a NATS message, and what is each component used for?<br>3. Which SDK should your service use, and how is it added as a dependency? |
| **04** | Client Connectivity | [Connection Options](client/nats-client-con-opts.md) | Enterprise standard for configuring client connections: externalized options, resilience, liveness, and graceful shutdown. | 1. What connection options must every service configure according to the enterprise standard?<br>2. How does your service stay connected and continue operating during broker failures or network blips?<br>3. What is the correct way to shut down a NATS connection without dropping in-flight messages? |
| **05** | Resource Governance | [Resource Lifecycle Management](nats-resources/resource-management.md) | Enterprise governance framework for Subjects, Streams, and Consumers: resource relationships, key platform invariants, ownership, approval workflows, and lifecycle states from request to retirement. | 1. What are the key relationships and invariants connecting Subjects, Streams, and Consumers in the platform model?<br>2. What responsibilities belong to the Application Team versus the Platform/SRE Team during resource onboarding and modification?<br>3. What are the lifecycle states of a NATS resource, and what prerequisites are required before a resource can be retired?<br>4. Why must resource modifications and retirements be assessed for downstream dependencies, and how are exceptions handled? |
| **06** | Subject Design | [Subject Guide](subject-guide.md) | Enterprise standard for designing NATS subjects: naming conventions, token hierarchy, wildcard rules, JetStream alignment, and versioning. | 1. What is the approved enterprise subject structure, and what does each token represent?<br>2. What naming rules apply to subject tokens, and what are the common anti-patterns to avoid?<br>3. When should you use a wildcard in a subscription, and why must publishers always use a fully specified subject?<br>4. How should your subject hierarchy account for JetStream stream and consumer filtering from the start?<br>5. When does a subject need versioning, and what is the correct way to introduce a new subject version? |
| **07** | Resource Provisioning | [Subject Intake](nats-resources/subject-intake.md) | Formal intake process for requesting, modifying, or retiring NATS subjects, including persistence justifications, stream mappings, and platform governance. | 1. What details must your team provide when requesting a new subject or modifying an existing one?<br>2. How do you justify whether your subject requires persistence, and who determines the underlying stream mapping?<br>3. What lifecycle and dependency considerations must be addressed before renaming or retiring a subject? |
| **08** | Core Messaging | [Core NATS Guide](nats-core-guide.md) | In-memory messaging patterns: Publish-Subscribe, Request-Reply (RPC), and Queue Group load balancing. | 1. For your use case, is Core NATS the right fit, or does it require persistence and guarantees that only JetStream provides?<br>2. How does each messaging pattern work: fan-out Publish-Subscribe, synchronous Request-Reply, and competing-consumer Queue Groups?<br>3. What are the throughput advantages and the trade-offs of in-memory messaging? |
| **09** | Persistent Messaging | [JetStream Guide](nats-jetstream-guide.md) | Persistence engine: stream creation and configuration, guaranteed publish receipts, retention rules, and Pull vs. Push consumer patterns. | 1. What does JetStream add on top of Core NATS, and what business requirements justify its use?<br>2. How do Streams work, and how do storage type, retention policy, and discard policy shape message lifecycle?<br>3. What is a Consumer, and how do you decide between a Pull Consumer and a Push Consumer for your workload?<br>4. What acknowledgment and delivery policies are available, and how do they affect message behavior? |
| **10** | Consumer Design | [Consumer Guide](nats-consumer-guide.md) | Enterprise standards for designing, naming, filtering, lifecycle governance, acknowledgement policies, and consuming messages from provisioned JetStream consumers across Go, Java, and Python. | 1. What is the approved naming format for durable consumers, and why must timestamps and pod/instance IDs be avoided?<br>2. How do subject filters ensure targeted message routing within parent stream boundaries?<br>3. Why do application services only bind to ("get") existing provisioned consumers rather than creating or updating them in code?<br>4. How does your application consume from a provisioned consumer and signal message success (`Ack`), retry (`Nak`), or permanent failure (`Term`)?<br>5. How do delivery policies determine starting position, and how do you request consumer access via the intake process? |
| **11** | Resource Provisioning | [Consumer Intake](nats-resources/consumer-intake.md) | Formal intake process for provisioning, modifying, or retiring platform-managed JetStream consumers, specifying subject filters, delivery modes, and acknowledgement criteria. | 1. What consumption requirements (subject filters, delivery model, and replay policy) must you define when requesting a consumer?<br>2. What is required when requesting a Push Consumer that is not needed for a Pull Consumer?<br>3. How do your processing success criteria and failure handling requirements translate into consumer configuration?<br>4. What impact analysis is required when modifying or retiring an existing consumer with active dependencies? |
| **12** | Delivery Semantics | [At-Most-Once Delivery](nats-delivery-semantics/atmost-once.md) | Best-effort fire-and-forget messaging over Core NATS: no persistence, no redelivery, optimized for low-latency transient workloads. | 1. What is At Most Once delivery, and what kinds of workloads is it the right choice for?<br>2. How does the message move from publisher to subscriber in this pattern?<br>3. What are the failure scenarios, and what does the application give up by choosing this pattern? |
| **13** | Delivery Semantics | [At-Least-Once Delivery](nats-delivery-semantics/atleast-once.md) | Guaranteed JetStream delivery with persistent storage, explicit consumer acknowledgments, automatic redelivery, and idempotency requirements. | 1. What is At Least Once delivery, and what does it guarantee that Core NATS cannot?<br>2. How does the acknowledgment lifecycle work, including success, failure, and redelivery paths?<br>3. Why must applications implementing this pattern handle duplicate messages, and how should they approach that? |
| **14** | Delivery Semantics | [Exactly-Once Delivery](nats-delivery-semantics/exacty-once.md) | Deduplicated end-to-end delivery combining publisher-side Nats-Msg-Id deduplication windows with consumer-side DoubleAck server confirmation. | 1. What is Exactly Once delivery in NATS, and what specific problem does it solve beyond At Least Once?<br>2. How does publisher deduplication using Nats-Msg-Id prevent duplicate entries in the stream?<br>3. What does consumer DoubleAck do, and why is it required to complete the exactly-once guarantee?<br>4. What does this pattern not cover, and where does the application still need its own safeguards? |
